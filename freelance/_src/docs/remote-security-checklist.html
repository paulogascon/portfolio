Remote work security checklistAccounts and devices
01 — The basics

Protect the accounts.
Then the devices.

A remote assistant often holds keys to several businesses at once. Most incidents start with a reused password or an unlocked device, so these two areas come first.

1

Accounts

  • Use a password manager and a unique password for every account. Long passphrases beat short complex ones.
  • Turn on multi-factor authentication everywhere it is offered. Prefer an authenticator app or a passkey over SMS codes.
  • Store recovery codes offline, somewhere you can reach if your phone is lost.
  • Never share a login in chat or email. Use the client's user invitations, delegation or a shared password-manager vault.
2

Devices

  • Keep automatic updates on for Windows, Android, browsers and apps.
  • Use a screen lock with a short timeout on the laptop and the phone.
  • Turn on encryption: BitLocker or Device Encryption on Windows; Android phones with a screen lock are encrypted by default on current versions.
  • Use a separate browser profile for each client, so sessions, extensions and saved data stay apart.
  • Don't let family members use the work profile or the work device.

Remote work security checklistEmail and client data
02 — Day to day

Slow down on email.
Share less data.

3

Email and messages

  • Check the sender's real address, not just the display name.
  • Hover over links before clicking, and open logins by typing the site address yourself.
  • Treat urgency, secrecy and payment changes as warning signs. Confirm any new bank detail or gift-card request through a second channel you already trust.
  • Never read out or forward a one-time code, even to someone who says they are from support.
4

Client data

  • Ask for the least access that does the job, and say which access you no longer need.
  • Keep client files in the client's own drive or workspace rather than personal storage.
  • Follow a 3-2-1 backup habit for your own work: three copies, on two kinds of storage, one kept off-site or in the cloud.
  • At the end of an engagement, hand back files, sign out of every session and ask the client to remove your access.
5

Networks

  • Change the router's admin password and use WPA2 or WPA3 encryption at home.
  • On public Wi-Fi, use your phone's hotspot for client systems instead.

03 — Incident steps

Clicked something?
Act in this order.

Step 1

Disconnect

Turn off Wi-Fi on the affected device. Don't keep typing passwords on it.

Step 2

Tell the client

Say what happened and when, right away. Early notice limits damage.

Step 3

Reset access

From a clean device, change the passwords involved and sign out other sessions.

Step 4

Write it down

Note the time, the message or link, and what you changed. Keep it with the client's records.

Monthly self-check

  • Updates installed on laptop and phone.
  • Password manager shows no reused or breached passwords.
  • MFA is on for email, cloud storage and every client account.
  • Backups ran, and one file was test-restored.
  • Old access from finished projects has been removed.
  • Browser extensions reviewed; unused ones removed.
  • Shared links to client files are limited to named people.
  • Recovery codes are still where you can find them.
Note

This checklist is general guidance for individual remote workers. Follow your client's own security policies where they are stricter.
